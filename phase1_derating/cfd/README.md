# CFD v2 — air-side heater unit cell (OpenFOAM)

**Purpose:** validate v1.5's *assumed* air-side heater UA(ṁ) scaling (UA∝ṁ⁰·⁶) against a real
CFD solve of the finned-tube geometry, then feed the CFD-validated law back into the cycle model
to produce the v2 derating curve. This is the physics-grounded replacement for the guess v1.5 had
to make about the hot-side heat exchanger.

**Path actually taken — "Option 1":** a single, well-instrumented CFD point at the cycle design
Reynolds number, validated against published correlations, rather than a multi-point Re sweep. Once
the single point validated heat transfer (§ Validation below), a sweep would have refined the same
Briggs–Young Nu(Re) shape at extra compute cost for a curve the sensitivity analysis (§ v2 result)
shows barely moves. A full CFD-derived Nu(Re) fit + a friction-domain fix (see Limitations) remain a
documented future extension, not a gap in the current result.

## How to run

All OpenFOAM commands run inside the ESI OpenFOAM Docker image (`opencfd/openfoam-default:2406`) via
`run/of.sh`, which mounts the `cfd/` tree at `/cfd` in the container. **Docker/entrypoint quirk:** the
image's `ENTRYPOINT` `cd`s to `$HOME` before running the given command, so a plain `docker run -w`
working-directory flag is silently ignored. `of.sh` works around this by `cd`-ing to the case
directory *inside* the shell command it passes to the container (derived from the caller's cwd
relative to the `cfd/` root), not via `-w`.

```bash
# from anywhere under phase1_derating/cfd/
run/of.sh blockMesh                 # run a single OpenFOAM command in the case dir
run/Allrun                          # full pipeline: mesh -> checkMesh -> parallel solve -> reconstruct

# post-processing / validation (pure Python, no Docker)
cd phase1_derating/cfd
python3 -m validation.single_point_check     # CFD vs Briggs-Young/Robinson-Briggs at Re=8368
python3 -m postprocessing.fit                # writes results/cfd_correlation.json
python3 cfd_to_v2.py                         # injects the UA law into v1.5 -> derating_curve_v2.csv

python3 -m pytest -q                         # full test suite (21 tests)
```

## The case

Staggered finned-tube unit cell (single tube, 3 fin pitches spanwise), steady compressible
`rhoSimpleFoam` + k-ω SST, meshed with snappyHexMesh, run in the OpenFOAM ESI image
(`opencfd/openfoam-default:2406`) via Docker.

- **Geometry:** tube OD 25.4 mm; fin height 12 mm, thickness 0.5 mm, pitch 4 mm; transverse pitch
  S_T = 2.0·d₀, longitudinal pitch S_L = 1.75·d₀ (staggered bank).
- **Boundary conditions:** isothermal tube wall 1033 K; air inlet 740 K, ≈2 bar; temperature-dependent
  air (Sutherland viscosity, JANAF cp).
- **Domain axes:** x = transverse → `cyclicX`; y = streamwise → `inlet`/`outlet`; z = tube axis →
  `symmetryPlane` at exact mid-fin-gap planes (spanwise closure — see Limitations for why this, not
  a streamwise-cyclic bundle, was used).
- **Mesh:** 3.29M cells; `checkMesh` OK (max non-orthogonality 64.8, max skewness 3.05).
- **Convergence:** SIMPLE converged in 464 iterations, all residuals < 1e-4; y⁺ mean 0.244, max 4.63
  on the tube wall (well-resolved boundary layer); temperature field physical (limiter dormant).
- **Design point:** Re = 8368 (tube-OD, minimum-flow-section mass flux).

## Validation — CFD vs published correlations (Re = 8368)

| Quantity | CFD | Correlation | Deviation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nu (heat transfer, LMTD) | 45.9 | 54.1 (Briggs–Young) | **−15.2%** | within ±20% → **air-side heat-transfer model VALIDATED** |
| f (friction) | 1.688 | 0.287 (Robinson–Briggs) | **+489%** | outside ±20% → **does NOT validate** |

The friction miss is not a Reynolds-dependence issue a sweep would resolve: the CFD Δp is measured
across the whole unit-cell domain (inlet plenum + tube bundle + outlet wake, ≈1.7 velocity heads),
while Robinson–Briggs describes only the incremental per-row bundle loss in a fully developed
multi-row bank — the two are measuring different things. Fin clipping (below) also adds spurious
form drag on the CFD side. Heat transfer, by contrast, validates cleanly because it is dominated by
the well-resolved bulk boundary layer (mean y⁺ 0.244), not by the entrance/exit geometry.

## The UA(ṁ) law injected into v2

Heat transfer being validated, the CFD-validated Briggs–Young Nu(Re) law (Nu ∝ Re^0.681) is adopted
as the air-side model and converted to an overall heater UA(ṁ), anchored to v1.5's design magnitude
**UA = 152.35 kW/K at ṁ_des = 54.37 kg/s** (so v2 reproduces v1.5 exactly at the design point).

The raw heat-transfer coefficient scales steeper than v1.5's assumed exponent (h ∝ Re^0.681 vs the
assumed 0.6), but UA = h·A·η_o, and the reference fin is thermally **inefficient**
(η_fin ≈ 0.53 at design — realistic for a low-conductivity, k ≈ 25 W/m·K, high-temperature alloy fin).
Overall surface efficiency η_o *falls* as h (and hence ṁ) rises — 0.62 → 0.54 across the swept range,
dln η_o/dln ṁ ≈ −0.23 — and that modulation dominates the raw Reynolds scaling:

**n_air_effective = 0.681 − 0.23 ≈ 0.454 — SHALLOWER than v1.5's assumed 0.6**, not steeper.

Friction did NOT validate, so the Robinson–Briggs Δp(ṁ) scaling in `postprocessing/fit.py`
(`dp_of_mdot`) is emitted into `cfd_correlation.json` as **informational only** — it is *not* wired
into the v2 power balance.

## v2 result — confirms v1.5

Injecting the CFD-validated UA(ṁ) law (n_air = 0.454) into the v1.5 cycle model:

| Ambient | v2 net MWe | % of 25 °C | Regime |
|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| 25 °C | 5.000 | 100.0% | B |
| 35 °C | 4.926 | 98.5% | B |
| 45 °C | 4.852 | 97.1% | A |
| 55 °C | 4.566 | 91.3% | A |

Mean electric derating 0.15%/°C (25–45 °C); **penalty at 55 °C = −8.7%**, vs v1.5's −8.8%
(assumed UA ∝ ṁ⁰·⁶) — a **+0.08-point delta that CONFIRMS v1.5**.

**Why the exponent change (0.6 → 0.454) barely moves the curve:** the open-air Brayton's
ambient-driven air mass flow only varies ~4–5% over the full 25→55 °C range, so even a materially
different UA-scaling exponent is a second-order effect on the derating curve. The desert thermal
penalty is dominated by compressor-inlet thermodynamics (v1/v1.5), not by the precise air-side heat
exchanger law — the CFD's role was to confirm that assumption was safe, which it did.

## Honest limitations

1. **Fin clipping.** The reference fin's outer radius (24.7 mm) exceeds S_L/2 (22.2 mm), so the
   12 mm fins overrun the single-tube streamwise unit-cell box and are clipped at the inlet/outlet
   planes (~42% of the inlet plane is fin metal at fin z-levels). This is inherent to the reference
   geometry (fin height ≈ tube radius) combined with a single-tube box — one cell cannot represent
   the interleaving of staggered neighboring tubes' fins. It biases the friction measurement (adds
   spurious clipped-fin form drag); heat transfer still validates within tolerance (−15.2%).
2. **Friction is not validated and is not used.** The air-side Δp/parasitic-power estimate
   (`dp_of_mdot`, Robinson–Briggs-based) is carried in `cfd_correlation.json` for reference only.
3. **Mesh independence not formally studied.** A single validated point stands in for a grid-convergence
   study — supported by the −15.2% Nu agreement, y⁺ mean 0.244, and a clean `checkMesh` (adequacy
   evidence, not proof). A formal 3-level grid-convergence study is future work.
4. **ω bounding at fin tips.** A localized near-singular-corner artifact in the k-ω solve at the fin
   leading edges; it does not perturb the converged U/p/T fields, and the integrated h/f are
   bulk-dominated (mean y⁺ 0.244), so it does not affect the reported result.
5. **Option 1, not a full sweep.** This campaign is a single-point validation + correlation-driven
   curve, not a 6-point CFD Reynolds sweep. It was the efficient path once the single point validated
   heat transfer; a fully CFD-derived Nu(Re) correlation and a friction-domain fix (dedicated
   Δp planes bracketing the bundle, or a multi-row domain) remain a documented future extension.

See `phase1_derating/spec/A1_reference_spec_sheet.md` §4d for the numbers in spec-sheet form and
`DECISIONS_LOG.md` D12 for the full why/consequence of the Option-1 choice.
